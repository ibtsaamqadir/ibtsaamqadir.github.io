About

Making the decision to operate more precise

Pancreatic cysts are found incidentally in a large share of abdominal scans. A few will become invasive cancer; most never will. The Fukuoka and Kyoto consensus guidelines resolve that uncertainty using imaging features, cytology and markers such as CA19-9 — sensitively, but with poor specificity, so a substantial number of patients undergo a pancreatectomy that carries real morbidity and, for them, no benefit.

I develop models that read the modalities jointly rather than in sequence, trained to predict high-grade dysplasia and invasive disease, and to anticipate who recovers well after surgery. I work in the Kolbinger Lab at the Weldon School of Biomedical Engineering, alongside the Division of Surgical Oncology at Indiana University School of Medicine.

Around that clinical core I work on the conditions under which such a model can be trusted: whether it survives a change of scanner, whether a vision-language model can be talked out of the right answer by text embedded in a surgical video, and what evidence a regulator should require before any of it reaches a patient.

Research

Four themes, one translational problem

A model that stratifies pancreatic cyst risk is only useful if it holds up on another hospital's scanner, if a surgeon can trust what it shows during an operation, and if a regulator can be shown why it belongs near a patient.

Pancreatic Disease Management

Multimodal risk stratification for intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms and pancreatic cancer — predicting malignant progression from imaging, labs and clinical variables, and predicting textbook outcome after pancreatectomy.

IPMNMultimodal AIRisk stratificationMRI & CT

Surgical Data Science

Video understanding for minimally invasive surgery, vision-language models for intraoperative decision support, and the adversarial failure modes that come with putting a language interface into an operating room.

Regulatory Science

What it should take to approve a medical AI device: study requirements across jurisdictions, and the gap between a better discrimination metric and a better patient outcome.

Theoretical Machine Learning

Where learned systems break — the fundamental limits that bound large language models regardless of scale, and generalisation under distribution shift across scanners, sites and time.
